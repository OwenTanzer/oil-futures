HSN-20260804-0145 — Hormuz near-closed; Abqaiq damaged; bypasses saturated — Aug 4, 2026, 1:45 AM EDT


Changes since last report:
No prior HSN report exists in the destination Doc; this is the baseline snapshot. No category transitions are available.


Network concentration risk:
The network is concentrated on the Saudi East–West/Yanbu and UAE Habshan–Fujairah bypasses, both operating near capacity while Abqaiq is damaged and the Red Sea outlet faces Houthi interdiction. Correlated failure of Abqaiq, Yanbu/Bab el-Mandeb, or Fujairah would strand additional Gulf output; long restoration tails at Ras Laffan and Habshan, suspended BAPCO operations, and partial Basra loadings limit redundancy.


Node status:
Strait of Hormuz | Category 3 | Commercial traffic remains effectively closed and recent weekly transits fell to wartime lows amid vessel attacks, authorization risk, and renewed U.S.–Iran strikes. | No scalable maritime substitute exists; insurance and interdiction remain binding.
Abqaiq | Category 1 | Current-run satellite imagery reviewed by AP confirms damage at the oil-processing complex after drone attacks, with throughput impact not yet officially quantified. | East–West Pipeline feed concentration makes restoration critical to the main Saudi bypass.

Habshan–Fujairah pipeline and Fujairah port/storage | Category 4 | Fujairah and its tanker terminal are operating at full capacity while the UAE bypass runs near full load. | This is the only large Gulf-of-Oman outlet bypassing Hormuz; new parallel capacity is not expected before 2027.

Bahrain: Sitra refinery and Saudi–Bahrain pipeline | Category 1 | Sitra suffered confirmed strike damage and BAPCO operations remain suspended even though vessel movements have resumed on a limited basis. | Pipeline-fed Saudi crude cannot restore product supply until refinery operations restart.
Bab el-Mandeb/Red Sea approaches | Category 3 | This newly systemic chokepoint is impaired by a Houthi blockade claim and attacks on Saudi-linked tankers. | It is now the binding seaward exit for the overloaded Yanbu bypass.
Iraq–Turkey pipeline/Ceyhan | Category 4 | A new one-year export agreement is intended to raise northern flows as southern Gulf exports remain unreliable. | Capacity and ramp timing limit its ability to replace Basra volumes.

Changes since last report:
No category transitions. Materially, U.S.–Iran/Oman contacts produced talks only over a temporary safe-navigation route; AP reported nine Sunday transits, so Hormuz remains Category 3. Red Sea insurance constraints tightened: war-risk coverage is being suspended for Saudi-linked vessels and tanker traffic was reported down 40%, leaving Bab el-Mandeb in Category 3 because the impairment remains insurance/interdiction-led rather than confirmed node damage. Inchcape reports no port-status change.


Network concentration risk:
Concentration remains on the Saudi East–West/Yanbu and UAE Habshan–Fujairah bypasses. API estimates their loadings more than doubled to about 5.8 million b/d after prewar Hormuz carried 15–16 million b/d, leaving large unserved dependence on any Strait reopening. Correlated failure risk now runs from damaged Abqaiq through Yanbu to Bab el-Mandeb insurance and interdiction, while Habshan restoration and Fujairah capacity constrain the UAE branch. Ceyhan's planned 750,000 b/d minimum is material but cannot replace prewar Iraqi exports of about 3.5 million b/d.
